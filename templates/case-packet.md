# Part I — Reader Packet / Freeze before

## 0. Role

- Who are you?
- What can you actually decide?
- What can you not decide?
- Who else matters?

## 1. Minimum background

Only include context necessary to understand the decision.

Do not include:

- outcomes after Freeze;
- later standard labels;
- teleological language;
- mature causal explanations;
- modern analogies that preload the answer.

## 2. Narrow timeline

Build only the path needed to reach Freeze.

## 3. Reader-visible evidence

Prefer contemporaneous P-evidence.

For each item, keep clear:

- date;
- source identity;
- what the actor could actually know;
- what remains inference.

If later recollection is necessary, label it explicitly as retrospective.

## 4. Stakes and constraints

- What is at risk?
- Which costs are borne by the actor?
- Which costs are borne by others?
- Which constraints are hard vs soft?

## 5. Known / unknown

### Known

### Known unknowns

### Unavailable information

Do not tell the reader which unknown later turned out to matter most.

## 6. Freeze

Use an exact Freeze only if evidence supports it. Otherwise use a Freeze Window or operational Freeze and say so.

## 7. Decision task

Avoid a simplistic multiple choice unless historically necessary.

Ask the reader to specify, where relevant:

- action / policy / route;
- scope;
- trigger threshold;
- review time;
- authority path;
- downside risk;
- what evidence would change the decision;
- confidence level.

# STOP

Do not continue until the reader has committed to a decision.

# Part II — Reveal / Review

## 9. Actual choice

State what happened without calling it correct or incorrect.

## 10. Execution and immediate feedback

Separate policy/decision quality from execution quality.

## 11. Medium- and long-run consequences

Separate:

- consequences plausibly linked to the choice;
- responses by others;
- environmental changes;
- luck;
- second-order effects.

## 12. Later evidence and historiography

Introduce mature historical research only here.

Do not turn Reveal into a single official answer. Preserve competing interpretations when material.

## 13. Decision quality vs outcome quality

Have the reader score separately:

- decision quality using Freeze information;
- outcome quality using what happened later.

Ask explicitly whether outcome knowledge changed the judgment of the original decision.

## 14. Transfer questions

Use structure, not superficial analogy. Ask which parts recur in ordinary life, organizations, relationships or risk management.
